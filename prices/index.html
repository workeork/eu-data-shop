What do these numbers mean?

These are negotiated rates, not your bill

Each price is the amount an insurer has agreed to pay an in-network provider for that service. It is the total the provider gets: the insurer's share plus your share. Low means the 10th percentile, typical is the middle (median), and high is the 90th percentile of all in-network providers we found in your state.

So what will I pay?

That depends on your plan. Before you meet your deductible, you usually pay the full negotiated rate. After the deductible, you pay your coinsurance, often 10–30% of the rate, or a fixed copay. Once you reach your out-of-pocket maximum you pay nothing more that year. Your insurer's member website or the number on your ID card can tell you exactly.

Why can one service have two prices?

Some services have a doctor's fee (the professional who does or reads the test) and a separate facility fee (the hospital or center). If both are shown, you may get two bills. Hospital outpatient departments often charge more than independent clinics.

Is this my exact plan?

Not necessarily. Insurers publish one file per network. We include the networks listed below. Your plan may use a different network with different prices. Out-of-network care is not included and is usually much more expensive.
